This paper proposes a multi-stage computer-aided detection (CAD) pipeline for microcalcifications (MCs) in digital mammograms. Breast cancer is among the leading causes of death in women, and MCs — tiny bright spots as small as 0.1mm — are early indicators of malignancy. The method begins with fuzzy C-means clustering for gross breast-region segmentation, followed by top-hat transform to localize the region of interest, and a watershed transform for precise isolation. Gibbs random field clique patterns are then used to detect MC candidates via adaptive thresholding. The algorithm achieves an overall detection rate of **94.4%**, sensitivity of **93.7%**, and detection accuracy of **88.2%**, with a false-negative rate of only 5.6%.
